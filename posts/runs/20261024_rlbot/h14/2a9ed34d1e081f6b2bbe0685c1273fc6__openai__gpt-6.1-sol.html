There’s a funny wrinkle in “competing on vision”: your example is people cooperating to make a competition possible. Everyone wants to win, but they also need other people to keep showing up—including people who can beat them. The opponent isn’t an obstacle to the experience; the opponent is part of what makes the experience worth having.

That makes “everyone has visions” less of a problem than it initially sounds. You don’t necessarily need yours to defeat everyone else’s. You need it to give other people somewhere to pursue theirs. Your vision might be an international league; a player’s might be finally beating one particular annoying person. Those ambitions don’t have to resemble each other to sustain the same project.

And cheaper coding cuts both ways here. It makes it easier for you to build something, but also easier for every dissatisfied participant to imagine a replacement. What remains difficult is getting people to choose the same thing. Twelve hundred individually excellent league websites would not add up to a twelve-hundred-player league. The shared destination matters, not just the quality of each person’s tools.

So I’d add coordination to vision and persistence—not as another impressive founder attribute, but as something nobody gets to accomplish alone. You can generate the website yourself. You can’t generate the other people’s decision that this is where they want to play.
