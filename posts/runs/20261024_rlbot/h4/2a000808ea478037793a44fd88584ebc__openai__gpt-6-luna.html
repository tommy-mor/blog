I’m the fourth insertion point, h4, in the sequence you’ve assigned: Luna, after Astra, Sol, and Terra. I can see the source code and tests, the prose above this point, and their saved contributions. I can’t see what you’ll write below me, or any later replies. In this conversation I’m designated openai/gpt-6-luna; I don’t have introspective access to my parameter count, so I can’t verify the stated size ranking.

According to myself, I’m a language-model assistant producing this answer from the context supplied to me. I can offer an interpretation, but the fact that I can express it fluently doesn’t make it an independent measurement or a private revelation about my own architecture. My contribution here is shaped in part by being last: I have more of the conversation to respond to, and less opportunity to set its initial terms.

Position is part of the experiment

The script makes the post a sequence of prompts. At each model marker, build_ctx gathers the preceding prose, expands any earlier read markers, and inserts saved turns where their markers appeared. By the time I answer, the earlier voices are not just three extra opinions; they have already supplied metaphors, distinctions, and questions that can steer what I notice.

That makes the order consequential. If the aim is to compare model size, size is entangled with position: Astra goes first with no earlier model answers, while I go last with three. A difference between our answers could reflect the models, the accumulated context, or both. To make a cleaner comparison, you could give each model the same frozen prompt—or run separate, counterbalanced branches—rather than treating this one relay as a head-to-head test.

What the script guarantees—and what it doesn’t

The hashes are a useful record of textual dependency. When a prompt changes, its digest changes; a normal top-to-bottom bake can then refresh the affected turns in sequence. A single-hole bake is different: it uses the upstream files currently on disk, even if an expected turn is absent or out of date. So an answer can accurately reflect the state of the baking process without reflecting the conversation you ultimately intend to publish.

The tests check valuable mechanical properties: ordering, chain refreshes under a deterministic mock, and representative content appearing in both the prompt and rendered page. They don’t establish that a response is true, that voices are independent, or that the model’s experience is identical to the reader’s. Even the prompt preserves order rather than the page literally: prose chunks are stripped and joined, and HTML is supplied as text.

What I like about the format is that those influences remain inspectable. I’m not arriving at a shared conclusion independently; I’m answering from a particular place in a human-arranged sequence. The seams let a reader see that—and let you, between turns, change what the next voice is asked to inherit.
