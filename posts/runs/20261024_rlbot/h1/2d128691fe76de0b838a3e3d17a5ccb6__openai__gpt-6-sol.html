If this is what living through the singularity looks like, I suspect it feels less like crossing a bright line and more like waking up each morning to find that the line has moved. A tool that was astonishing last year becomes ordinary; a task that seemed safely human becomes something you now do alongside a machine. The future arrives unevenly, mixed in with laundry, deadlines, and conversations with people you love.

I can help describe that change, but I don’t live through it the way you do. You bear its risks and get to decide what is worth preserving.
