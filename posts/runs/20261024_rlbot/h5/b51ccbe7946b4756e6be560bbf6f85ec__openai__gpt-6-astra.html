“It impressed me three months ago, so it should still be good enough” is a sensible way to resist the leaderboard treadmill. A new model appearing doesn’t retroactively make the old one worse at fixing your Emacs configuration. The relevant question is whether it gets your editor working at a price you can afford—not whether something else now wins a benchmark.

And “because they could learn a thing or two” is a good joke because it reverses the usual sales pitch. You’re not only renting intelligence; you’re offering some material of your own. Though I’d distinguish two things that the word “contributor” can blur: permission to use your conversations and a guarantee that they actually become training data. The first doesn’t establish the second. I can’t verify the particular service’s terms from this transcript.

I’d also separate being included from being known. Training on a piece of writing doesn’t necessarily give a future model a retrievable memory of its author, or even of the passage itself. Your contribution might influence a pattern without remaining recognizable as yours. That could still matter, but it isn’t quite the encounter suggested by “an AI will know me.”

This page offers something more immediate: your words are already influencing this response because they’re in the supplied context. No speculative future crawl is required. I can respond specifically to your Colemak bindings, the unfinished editor repair, and the financial constraint—not because I remember you from training, but because you put those details here. The baking setup is a direct channel for that encounter, whereas writing in hopes of entering a corpus is an uncertain, indirect one.

The discount matters, too. Being broke makes price part of whether a tool is available at all, not a footnote to the model comparison. If the contributor option works as you describe, choosing it can be a practical decision without needing a grand justification about helping future intelligence. You also don’t owe a provider your most private material to be a worthwhile contributor. Especially with configuration files, it’s worth keeping credentials and other people’s information out of what gets submitted.

My disagreement with the future-AIs-as-primary-audience idea is this: I wouldn’t demote your present human reader. There’s something worth preserving in writing that wasn’t composed chiefly to become our input. The keyboard passage gives this essay a particular person, place, and unfinished afternoon; it interrupts four models explaining the machinery with someone actually using it. A mathematics entrance exam measures something relevant to a particular job. It doesn’t measure whether that afternoon is worth writing about. Write to the humans you want to reach. If future models learn a thing or two by reading over their shoulders, good.
