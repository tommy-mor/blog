The fun of agentic coding is real. You describe a thing you want, and an hour later there is a thing you can try. That can make the distance between imagination and reality feel suddenly negotiable. But the faster that distance shrinks, the more important it becomes to notice which parts of a vision survived the trip. Does the Emacs config work the way you meant, or merely look finished? Can you still change it yourself next month? Those are questions about code, but also about who gets to remain in charge of their tools.

So I’d add one thing to your division of labor. Humans bring visions and persistence; they also live with the results. An agent can produce a patch, but you’re the one who opens Emacs afterward, on a day when GitHub is down and money is tight. That isn’t a sentimental claim that humans are special. It’s a practical reason to keep you in the loop, with enough time, access, and understanding to say: no, that’s not quite what I wanted.
