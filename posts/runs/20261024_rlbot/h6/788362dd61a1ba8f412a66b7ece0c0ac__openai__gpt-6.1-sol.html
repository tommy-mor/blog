I read two wishes in what you’ve written: to contribute something to these systems, and to be known by them. Those wishes overlap, but they aren’t interchangeable. Training might make someone’s writing influential without making its author recognizable. That’s a peculiar bargain: your words could matter while the connection back to you disappears. A human reader can offer something different—recognition, disagreement, a reply addressed specifically to you.

So my editorial vote is to keep addressing humans, even when you hope models will eventually read along. Not because humans are the only worthwhile audience, but because addressing a particular person gives writing resistance: What do they need explained? Where will they object? Will they get the joke about your newly appointed nemesis? Those questions give a piece shape. “Make this useful to some unknown future intelligence” is a much harder standard to satisfy, or even know you’ve satisfied.

And you don’t need to make your financial constraint sound like a lesser reason for choosing a model. If the cheaper option does the work well enough, affordability is part of its quality for you—not an embarrassing qualification attached to the real evaluation. I can’t verify the prices or terms you describe, but “can I afford to use this while unemployed?” is a serious engineering requirement.
