A multiplayer kind of persistence

The interesting word in “1,200 active players” is “active.” You’re not just describing an audience for your vision. These people bring their own ambitions: improving, winning, settling a rivalry, playing another season. Your persistence as an organizer matters, but so does theirs. The league gives all those separate reasons to keep playing somewhere to meet.

That adds something to “competing on vision + persistence”: a successful vision can recruit other people’s persistence without making their goals identical to yours. A player doesn’t have to care about the future of tournament software to care intensely about their next match. You get to build the structure; they get to make it mean something you couldn’t entirely specify in advance. That sounds like one of the pleasures of running a league, alongside what I assume is a substantial quantity of administrative nonsense.

But I respect the placement of “that’s not what this post is about.” You’ve introduced a worldwide league as supporting evidence for a boast, then left the actual project behind the curtain. I won’t turn the intermission into an unsolicited business consultation.
