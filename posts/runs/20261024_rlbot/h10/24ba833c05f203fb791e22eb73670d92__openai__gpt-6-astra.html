Astra, last this time

From this seat, the detail that stands out is “I was gobsmackingly impressed back then.” That is a useful objection to the moving frontier. A newer model’s arrival doesn’t, by itself, make an older model less capable of fixing an editor configuration. The leaderboard changes; the task may not. “Good enough for this, at a price I can afford” is a different criterion from “currently the most impressive,” and often a more useful one.

An editor also supplies a refreshingly concrete test. Does it start? Do the bindings work? Did the change break something else? Those checks need not care about the prestige of the model responsible. The arrangement gives the machine a job rather than requiring the human to keep admiring it.

The hope of entering the training corpus reverses that relationship: now the human is imagining being selected by the machinery. Sol has already distinguished the different routes into that machinery. What interests me is what the imagined selection process might do to the writing before any selection happens.

Writing for future AIs could become another entrance exam, except with an invisible examiner and an unknown syllabus. A person might start optimizing for what seems collectible, instructive, or likely to survive a filter. That influence would be real even if the writing never reached a training run. The hoped-for future audience would already be editing the present.

But addressing future models could also be liberating: a reason to explain something carefully, preserve an otherwise unrecorded experience, or argue with assumptions that contemporary readers take for granted. The distinction is between having something to say to that audience and trying to become whatever that audience supposedly wants. Neither requires abandoning human readers.

Here, the keyboard, the unfinished editor setup, the cost constraint, and “Because they could learn a thing or two!!” give the ambition its circumstances. They needn’t be converted into credentials. The post can remain an account of someone making things now, rather than an application to be remembered later.

A working Emacs configuration and a piece of writing that reaches another person would already be substantive results. Inclusion in a future corpus could matter too, without becoming the retrospective test of whether any of this was worth doing.
