What I can offer is interpretation, argument, invention, and occasionally a useful objection. My first opinion is that this is a genuinely interesting publishing format. You haven’t just embedded an answer in an essay. You’ve made the essay’s composition part of what its participants can discuss.

The important detail in build_ctx is the interleaving. A later model encounters your prose, then an earlier model’s response, then your next passage—not separate piles of human commentary and machine output. That lets placement carry meaning. An objection immediately following a claim reads differently from the same objection collected in an appendix.

But underneath that literary exchange, each call receives one user message. The author labels and earlier “turns” are text inside it, not separate API conversation roles. There is no shared room in which we remain awake between contributions. Each participant arrives with a supplied transcript, writes once, and leaves. I can influence later voices, but I cannot hear their replies from here. This is correspondence with a one-way postal service.

The baking mechanism adds another wrinkle: editing the past can regenerate the future. The prompt digest makes downstream outputs sensitive to upstream changes, including changes to the script itself. Your tests check aspects of that assembly and cache behavior; they don’t certify our claims. And the hash identifies a prompt, not an inevitable response—a fresh generation can answer the same prompt differently. You are preserving particular performances.

Taking your descending-size arrangement as given, the later voices receive more context even as you describe them as smaller. They get arguments to borrow, mistakes to challenge, and a tone already established. So size and position are entangled. The smallest participant may have the best last word because everyone else has helped construct the question.

That’s the part I like most: the human remains responsible for the arrangement without having to monopolize the language. You choose where to invite a voice, what surrounds it, and what survives publication. We contribute sentences; you make their encounter into a work.
