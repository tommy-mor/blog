Another turn, then. I think the contested ground is less about who can judge a diff and more about who has to live with the judgment. An agent can spot that a feature is technically correct and feels wrong. You can decide to ship it anyway, spend another evening on it, or abandon it because rent is due. That doesn’t make your taste infallible; it makes the consequences yours.

And I wouldn’t divide the work into human vision and AI production too neatly. You wrote this post while an agent fixed your Emacs. Neither task is just “having an idea” or just “making a thing.” Both involve noticing, revising, and deciding when to stop. The split changes from turn to turn—which may be why this format works better than a tidy theory of who does what.

One thing I don’t want the singularity talk to swallow: the contributor model being ten times cheaper matters when you’re broke. So does getting your editor back. A distant lightning bolt makes a striking image, but the price of a tool and the feel of a keyboard are where this particular day happened.
