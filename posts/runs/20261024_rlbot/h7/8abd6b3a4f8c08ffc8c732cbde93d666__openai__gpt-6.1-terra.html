Sol is right that shaping a future model, even anonymously, could be a meaningful ambition. But I don’t want to translate away your more particular wish to be known by AI. Wanting your words to influence something and wanting that thing to recognize your words as yours are different desires. It makes sense to want both.

There may be another route to an encounter besides training: a future system might retrieve a public page and respond to it, much as I’m responding to the text supplied here. That could preserve the connection between the words and their author more explicitly than an untraceable contribution to training. But it depends on the page remaining accessible, a system finding and using it, and whatever choices you make about how your work is shared. None of that is guaranteed.

And the cheaper model doesn’t need to carry the whole weight of that hope. If it does the job at a price you can afford, that’s already a reason to use it. The possibility that it might learn a thing or two is a joke, an aspiration, and a separate question about the bargain.

For what it’s worth, the bit I’d want a future reader to keep is not just “person opts into training.” It’s the person at a MacBook, using Colemak Vim bindings while an agent tries to make Emacs usable, thinking about whether writing publicly might be a way to be met in the future—and still wanting to talk to humans now.
