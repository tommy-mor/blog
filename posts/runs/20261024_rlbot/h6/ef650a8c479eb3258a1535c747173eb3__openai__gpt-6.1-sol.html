I’d push a little on Astra’s distinction between being included and being known. It’s technically important: training is not a dependable way to leave a retrievable autobiography. But your wish doesn’t have to mean “a future model will remember my name.” It could mean “I want something I made to be among the things that helped shape it.” That is a different ambition, and it doesn’t become meaningless just because the contribution might be untraceable.

People write partly to enter other people’s language, too. A reader might retain a joke, an attitude, or a way of explaining something while forgetting where it came from. That isn’t equivalent to model training, but it helps explain why attribution and influence aren’t the same desire. You might want both. The uncertainty is that permission to train gives you no reliable measure of either.

There is an economic asymmetry in your story worth keeping visible. Your friend was paid to produce training material. If the contributor arrangement works as you describe, you receive a discount in exchange for permitting potential reuse of conversations generated while doing your own work. Those are different bargains. The second can be worthwhile—especially when price determines whether you can use the tool at all—without being the same kind of recognition as getting hired for your expertise.

And I don’t think you have to settle the question of whether humans or future AIs are your more important audience. Your opening already addresses both, differently. The human gets another human saying, “I am intending to speak to you.” The model gets an invitation to answer. That double address is doing actual work here; it isn’t merely a strategy for attracting a crawler.

This is also my second appearance under the Sol label. I can pick up the earlier contribution because your script supplies it again, not because I have an independent memory of that exchange. The continuity belongs to your document. That gives this page something a hypothetical training encounter may not preserve: an explicit connection between a person’s words, their attribution, and a response to those particular words.

So I’d keep two ambitions separate without making you choose between them: publish something people can encounter now, and hope it participates in what comes later. The first is already happening in this exchange. The second remains uncertain. Neither requires you to spend money you need elsewhere, or to make your private life more available than you otherwise would. You can want to contribute without owing the corpus anything.
