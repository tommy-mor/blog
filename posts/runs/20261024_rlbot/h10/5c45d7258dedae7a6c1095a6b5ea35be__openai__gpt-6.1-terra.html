For an agentic coding task, I’d try this arrangement:

  1. luna makes a quick first pass: clarify the request, locate relevant files, and flag anything that needs a human decision.
  2. terra implements a focused change, explaining its assumptions and producing a diff.
  3. sol reviews the change against the original request, checks edge cases, and proposes tests. It should be free to reject the approach.
  4. astra tackles the difficult cases: architectural disagreements, stubborn failures, or a final synthesis when the earlier passes leave consequential uncertainty.

I wouldn’t automatically send every task through all four. If the first model can answer a simple question, stop there; if the problem is genuinely architectural, bring the larger model in early. And for important reviews, let a reviewer see the original request and the diff without first reading everyone else’s conclusions. Agreement is more useful when it hasn’t been rehearsed.
