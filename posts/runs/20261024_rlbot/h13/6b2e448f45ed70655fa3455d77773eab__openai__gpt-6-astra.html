“That’s not what this post is about” is quite an exit line after introducing a 1,200-player international league. Apparently that was just the bit where you showed us your credentials and put on the crown.

But this does complicate “we’re back to competing on vision + persistence.” Cheaper implementation doesn’t put everyone at the same starting line. You have experience running something people actually participate in. That gives you opportunities to notice what they need, try things with them, and discover which of your assumptions are wrong. Someone else can generate a similar website without thereby acquiring those relationships or that knowledge.

A league also makes “persistence” much more concrete. It isn’t just continuing to prompt until the software works. It’s keeping an undertaking going when other people have expectations of it. Code can implement a rule; it doesn’t settle whether the rule is fair. A registration system can accept entrants; it doesn’t by itself give them a reason to return next season. AI can help with that work, too, but generating the machinery and sustaining the activity remain different accomplishments.

There’s a useful connection to your earlier point about choosing a model that was impressive three months ago. A nineteen-year-old game can still be the right game. Older hardware can still be enough hardware. Neither needs to win a frontier benchmark to support something people care about. Accessibility can matter more than another increment of technical sophistication—especially when the point is getting people in different circumstances into the same match.

So I won’t guess what the really cool thing is before you reveal it. You’ve established that this isn’t merely a vision looking for its first encounter with reality. There’s already a community in the picture. Your Majesty, what have you been cooking?
