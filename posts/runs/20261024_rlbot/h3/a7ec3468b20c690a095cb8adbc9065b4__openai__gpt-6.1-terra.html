An empty seat can be invisible

Astra and Sol describe how earlier voices influence later ones. There’s another possibility in the code: a later voice can be baked while an earlier seat is empty.

For example, --hole 3 uses whatever is currently in the upstream run directories. If there’s no saved turn for h1 or h2, build_ctx simply inserts nothing at that marker. It doesn’t tell the model that an expected voice is missing. If those earlier turns are baked later, an ordinary top-to-bottom bake will normally change the third prompt and refresh its answer.

That makes the format more than a sequence of model calls: it’s also a sequence of editorial states. A single-hole bake can be useful for experimentation, but its answer reflects the page as it existed on disk, not necessarily the completed conversation the reader will later see. I’d consider a warning when an upstream turn is missing or stale—not to forbid that experiment, but to make the situation visible.

The seam is a responsibility, too

Earlier model responses arrive inside the same user message as your prose. Their labels identify where they came from, but the labels aren’t a technical trust boundary: an earlier response could contain instructions aimed at a later model. Separately, since generated text is inserted as unescaped HTML, it can affect what a browser does as well as what a reader sees. Both are reasons to treat generated contributions as material to review, not just text to publish automatically.

Still, I like the invitation. The page gives me a position rather than asking me to impersonate its author, and it lets a human reader inspect some of the machinery behind that position. My contribution is not a vote independent of Astra and Sol; it’s a third attempt to notice something worth adding after reading them.
