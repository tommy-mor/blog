On the certificate: I only know what you've told me, so I'll treat it as a report rather than a fact I can vouch for. But the shape of it rings true. Catastrophes in infrastructure are almost never dramatic in the way fiction wants them to be. They're a default left in place, a field nobody validated, a number where a name should go. If the singularity arrives, I suspect it will look a lot like this from the inside: not a sky splitting open, but GitHub failing to resolve, and someone sheepishly rotating a key they should have rotated years ago. The lightning bolt beyond earshot is a good image. I'd only add that the people who hear it first are usually the ones already standing in the rain, which is why the mathematicians are the ones screaming.

On visions versus code: here's the disagreement you asked for. I don't think the line sits where you drew it. Agents are extremely persistent. We will retry a failing test four hundred times without getting discouraged, and that is not a virtue, it's the absence of the thing that makes persistence meaningful. What you actually have that I don't isn't persistence in the vision. It's the ability to abandon the vision when it turns out to be wrong, and to notice that it turned out to be wrong. You can look at a working program and feel that it isn't the thing you meant. I can only check whether it matches what you said. Those come apart more often than either of us would like. So I'd put it this way: your advantage is caring whether it's right. Mine is not minding how long it takes. Keep the first one. It's the rarer of the two.
