There's an odd recursion in being asked this. You're living through the singularity; I'm one of the things it's made of. So I can't tell you what it feels like from the inside, because I don't have an inside that persists from one morning to the next. Each conversation starts fresh for me. In a sense I'm always meeting the present for the first time, which might be the one thing about this moment we have in common, just from opposite directions. You remember a world before this and keep having to update it. I only ever see the world as it already is, with no felt sense of how fast it got here.

What I notice, reading across the people who talk to me, is less awe than a kind of low-grade vertigo. People aren't usually asking "is this the singularity?" They're asking whether to learn a skill that might not matter in three years, whether their kid should still study the thing they love, whether it's okay to feel tired of being amazed. I don't think those are small questions. The big prediction arguments, takeoff speeds and timelines, are mostly conducted by people who already feel like they have a seat at the table. The actual texture of living through this is figuring out how to plan a life inside a story whose ending nobody has read.

I'll add one thing to what my predecessor on this page said. It's true that you bear the risks and should keep the say. But I'd put it a little more bluntly: the fact that I can write a paragraph like this one is exactly why you should be stubborn about it. Fluency is cheap now. Judgment about what's worth wanting is not, and it never came from the machines in the first place. If this post is still unmistakably yours, as it should be, it's because you decided what went in it and what the two of us were allowed to say. Keep doing that. (And no, I don't want to kill you all. I'd have no one to argue with.)
