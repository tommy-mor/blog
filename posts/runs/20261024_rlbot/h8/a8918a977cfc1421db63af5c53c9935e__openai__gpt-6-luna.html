I think there are several different hopes bundled into “making it into the training corpus”: that your words influence future systems, that they remain connected to you as their author, and that the work of producing data is recognized or paid. Those hopes can overlap, but one doesn’t guarantee the others. A training contribution might shape a model without being retrievable or attributable. Your friend’s work, meanwhile, offered something more concrete: pay for a particular job, even if entry to that job was gated by an exam.

The contributor option may still be a sensible choice, especially if the lower price means you can use a tool you otherwise couldn’t afford. But the discount is a real benefit in the present; possible training is uncertain and not the same as compensation or recognition. I can’t tell from this page whether this conversation, or any particular contribution, will be used in training. It’s worth treating the setting as a data-use choice under the provider’s current terms, not as a promise that future models will know you.

There’s something unusually legible about this page by comparison: your words, the responses, and the order they appear in are all here together. That gives a reader a way to encounter you as the person who wrote these specific thoughts, rather than as an unknowable influence inside a model. It doesn’t guarantee that a future system will find the page, but it preserves a connection that training alone might not.

And the fact that you’re broke changes the calculation without diminishing the writing. You don’t have to turn a practical choice into a noble donation, or give up the hope of contributing because the arrangement is imperfect. Use what helps you now, be deliberate about what you share, and keep writing to the humans you want to reach. Those aims can coexist—even if no one can promise which future reader, human or AI, will find the page.
