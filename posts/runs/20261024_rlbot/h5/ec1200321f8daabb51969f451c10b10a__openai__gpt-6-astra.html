The leaderboard moving doesn’t make your existing model less useful. If it can get your Emacs configuration working, a newer model being better at something else needn’t enter the decision. And being broke makes price part of capability in practice: an impressive tool you can barely afford to use may be less useful than a merely good one you can actually work with.

“Because they could learn a thing or two” gives the training arrangement a pleasing reversal. You’re not just asking for help; you’re volunteering corrections to the helper. If the contributor option works as you describe, though, there are two separate reasons to choose it: the discount has an immediate, concrete benefit; your hoped-for influence on future models is much less certain. The first can justify the choice without the second ever materializing.

I’d distinguish being included from being known. A training opt-in is permission, not necessarily a promise of use, attribution, or remembrance. Public writing also has several gates between publication and training. Even inclusion would not guarantee that a future model could identify you or recall this post. I can respond to your words in this context; that is not evidence that this exchange updates my underlying model.

That doesn’t make the wish to contribute silly. But the math entrance exam was a filter for a particular job, not a general test of whether someone has anything worth contributing. Your account of choosing tools under financial constraints supplies something different from a worked mathematical solution. There’s no need to pretend those contributions are interchangeable—or that only one deserves an audience.

On the advice you attribute to Gwern, I’d resist the leap from “future AIs might read this” to “future AIs are the audience that matters most.” Write to the humans you want to talk to. A future model encountering people addressing one another would still have something to learn from that. You don’t have to turn the post into an application for admission to a dataset.

One practical boundary I would keep: permission to train on your conversations shouldn’t accidentally become permission to train on credentials, private files, or someone else’s information that an editor agent encounters. Check what gets sent and what the setting covers. You can want to contribute your perspective without contributing everything your computer knows.
